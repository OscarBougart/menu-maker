# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Bar owners and venue operators who run craft cocktail bars. They open this tool when they need a new seasonal menu or want to refresh a look, and they don't have a graphic designer on call. They care deeply about how the menu looks because it represents the bar's identity at the table. They are not designers by training but they have strong taste — they know what they like when they see it.

Context of use: running locally in a terminal + browser, probably during a quiet afternoon at the bar or at home. Single-user, no onboarding pressure.

## Product Purpose

A local AI-powered cocktail menu design studio. The user uploads a photo of a menu they admire, the AI extracts its design system (palette, typography rules, aesthetic summary), they write a short brief, and the tool generates a styled print-ready menu they can edit inline and export as a PDF.

Two-tier memory: individual named style templates (e.g. "Death & Co", "Paradiso") and a growing House Brain that reconciles rules from multiple references into a master design system. Success looks like: a bar owner with no design background produces a menu that looks like it came from a professional print studio.

## Brand Personality

Craft, warmth, expertise. The studio should feel like a knowledgeable friend who makes beautiful things — not a software company, not a themed cocktail lounge. Quiet confidence. The tool knows what it's doing and doesn't need to announce it.

## Anti-references

- No generic SaaS: not rounded cards, not blue-primary button hierarchies, not dashboard-grid layouts. Should feel handcrafted, not enterprise-tooling.
- Not Canva or general-purpose design tools: not a blank-canvas creative platform. A specialist instrument for a specific job.
- Not the menus it generates: the studio UI should be a neutral dark workspace that lets the generated menu preview take center stage. If the UI has strong personality (heavy brass, blood-red CTAs, apothecary texture), it competes with the output. Pull back.

## Design Principles

1. **The tool steps back.** Studio chrome is quiet and receding. The menu preview in the canvas IS the product. Everything else is in service of it.
2. **Craft without costume.** Warmth and expertise through typographic precision and considered spacing — not through themed decoration. The tool earns its character; it doesn't wear it.
3. **Expertise shows in silence.** Minimal UI noise. Concise labels, not explanatory instructions. Trust the user. Friction is the enemy.
4. **Print-first thinking.** The output is a physical object. Every design decision — layout, color, font choice — should feel like it belongs in the context of a printed menu, not a web app.
5. **Earned detail.** Small touches (swatch rows, step numerals, italic summaries) feel placed deliberately. Nothing decorative for its own sake.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Best effort on the studio UI: reasonable contrast ratios, keyboard-navigable controls, no motion that can't be tolerated. No formal WCAG compliance target. Generated menus are print artifacts — their contrast is a design choice, not an accessibility requirement.
